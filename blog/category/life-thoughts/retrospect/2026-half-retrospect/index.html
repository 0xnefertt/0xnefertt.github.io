Introduction

Half of 2026 has passed. On January 1, I wrote a review of 2025 and set goals for the future. At the end of April, when a third of the year had gone by, I wrote another reflection to shake myself out of my laziness. This time, I thought I would write to wrap up the first half of the year. That makes three posts this year that could be called retrospectives. Writing one every quarter might be a good idea going forward. Blogging used to feel burdensome, but these days it feels easier and takes less time. Writing anonymously probably makes a big difference because I can write more freely. Separately, having ChatGPT give my writing a final review has greatly reduced my fear of writing.

I started this blog this year. Instead of using a platform such as Naver or Substack, I made a personal blog with a free domain. It is inconvenient. Attaching images to posts is especially tedious, as are categorizing and moving posts. The lack of communication with others is another drawback. I still do not know who reads this blog. Then who am I writing for? I do not really know that either. For now, my purpose is to leave a record. Perhaps that means I am writing for myself. I plan to move over all the posts I previously wrote elsewhere. I do not quite understand why I want to go this far despite the inconvenience. I do not know whether I am doing the right thing, either.

A Time of Upheaval

Since entering my thirties, I have encountered things I never expected and somehow kept dealing with whatever was in front of me. That is how I ended up living abroad. It had always been a vague possibility, never part of my plan. I need to accept reality, yet there are still many parts I have not accepted. My goal in my twenties was to buy my own home. Naturally, I had been focused solely on buying a home in Korea. I have made more than 130 payments into my housing subscription savings account and saved over KRW 20 million in it. Living abroad makes that dream feel a little farther away for now.

Because I keep dealing with what is right in front of me, I do not know what lies beyond it. Sometimes it seems endlessly far away; sometimes it feels like an illusion. The things I cannot handle and keep postponing simply pile up. They are things I will eventually have to resolve, but I avoid them because they are difficult, boring, or bothersome. English, coding, and exercise fall into this category. They take time; you do not suddenly become good at them overnight. Despite knowing better than anyone that consistency brings improvement, I do not do them. Like tidying my desk when exam season approaches, I keep trying to organize my priorities instead. I feel as if I will keep making excuses until everything eventually blows up. Only when it is too late to turn back will I regret it again…

This latest cycle brought intense FOMO as semiconductor stocks surged. The only Korean stocks I had held were Samsung, Hyundai Motor, Naver, and entertainment companies. Of course, I sold them all long ago and have not bought any since, so I did not suffer losses. Still, even watching from a distance, I felt enormous envy. It feels as though I have reached a crossroads in life. I keep feeling that I will not be able to live an ordinary life. As polarization worsens, I worry about where I will end up and whether I can endure and keep going.

Time to Throw a Stone Into a Calm Lake

“Korea is fast and convenient, but uneasy on the mind. Canada is inconvenient and slow, but peaceful on the mind.”

I once saw a post like this on Threads, and many people agreed.

It was a post I had simply scrolled past, but suddenly I thought it was wrong. When I first came to Canada, the weather was nice and I had no worries. I felt as if I were starting a new life. Perhaps I was conditioning myself to believe, “It is okay; I have only just arrived. Everything will work out later.” And for me to believe that things will improve and that I am happier now, my earlier memories have to be the opposite. Perhaps that is why I decided Korea was an uneasy place and judged Koreans as blunt while liking Canadians because they smiled at me. There are many people in the world, and just as many different kinds of people. Ultimately, whether your mind feels at ease depends on how you think.

Anyway, Canada feels as calm as a lake. Of course, considering the cost of living, it is not exactly stable or tranquil. But looking only at the nature and weather, the city certainly has its charm.

Now, though, it is time to throw a stone into that lake. I have to survive the ripples that gently spread across it. Only then can I understand how I am staying afloat and whether I am somewhere safe. A jet ski is better than a cheap wooden sailboat, and boarding a yacht or cruise ship would feel safer and more comfortable still. Let me keep assessing where I am and finding ways to upgrade.

Review

Looking back on the first half of the year, there were no particularly notable achievements apart from a major change in my mindset after visiting Korea in April.

I need to reflect on how little diligence I have shown toward my plans.

English study is at least one thing I have kept doing, and continuity seems to be what matters.

I signed up as a freelancer on Upwork to earn money on the side, but people give me assignments before signing a contract and then never reply. Still, I am trying to think positively: perhaps solving these assignments can help me improve my skills.

I should keep better records on the blog so I can review things more thoroughly in my next retrospective.

Plans Ahead

If it is something I will have to do eventually, let me stop putting it off and get it done quickly.

And let me hold firmly to what I planned and push it through to the end so it does not go off course.
