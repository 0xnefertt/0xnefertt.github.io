Once you have chosen your wedding date, you need to choose a venue.

**Wedding venues usually start taking bookings one year before the wedding.**

The places you saw on Instagram and thought, “_Wow, this is beautiful. I have to get married here!_” or venues so famous that everyone knows their names may already be fully booked even if you inquire a year ahead. So if there is a venue you really want, I recommend keeping in touch well in advance.

Before choosing a venue, I think there are four main things to consider.

1\. Budget

2\. Number of guests

3\. Location

4\. Atmosphere

It goes without saying that the budget matters most.

Wedding venue prices vary enormously because one extra charge leads to another, and then another.

That is why I think **the couple should start by setting a firm budget for how much they will spend on the wedding.**

Of course, you may have a dream wedding in mind.

It might be a once-in-a-lifetime event.

That sounds lovely.

But graduation was a once-in-a-lifetime event, too, and you only turned twenty once.

When I was in school, I wanted a mobile phone, and my parents said they would buy me one if I got good exam results.

I studied hard just because I wanted that phone, and when I finally got my first one, I was thrilled.

But that feeling did not last forever. Maybe about seven days after I got it?

Anyway, weddings are like that, too.

Looking at the reality of weddings in Korea today, I feel they are being given far more significance than we realize.

Besides, a wedding is about more than fulfilling a dream. It is also an event for both families' parents.

Sometimes more of the parents' acquaintances and relatives attend than the couple's own guests.

**Before booking a venue, make sure you check the guest count with your parents.**

That is also why location matters.

**When choosing a location, check whether transportation is convenient, how many cars can park, and how long guests can park for.**

Like me, many young people move from other regions to Seoul for work and live there, so many weddings take place in Seoul. Their colleagues and friends are there, too. Since family members travel up from other regions, a location near a train station or bus terminal is generally a good choice.

If the guests are somewhat older, many may drive to the venue. In that case, parking is an absolute must.

Finally, there is the atmosphere you want for your wedding.

The three points above are actually easier because you can assess them objectively.

Atmosphere, however, depends on personal taste, so it helps to decide on the style you want.

1\. Hotel wedding

2\. Wedding hall

3\. House wedding or small wedding

4\. Garden or outdoor venue

5\. Hanok or traditional venue

6\. Chapel- or cathedral-style venue

7\. Restaurant or dining wedding

And so on... There is a huge range.

Even conventional wedding halls differ depending on whether they have a bright or dark atmosphere, high or low ceilings, and a long or short wedding aisle. All of that changes how the space feels. That is what makes it difficult.

Flowers can especially transform the atmosphere of hotel, outdoor, and hanok weddings.

If you do not know what you like,

try using the following prompts to help.

A. Grand and classic

→ High ceilings, a long wedding aisle, chandeliers, a cathedral or hotel feel

B. Warm and charming

→ A house wedding, a small space, and a relaxed atmosphere with guests close by

C. Natural and bright

→ Outdoors, gardens, full-height windows, natural light, and plenty of greenery

D. Korean and refined

→ Hanok, historic homes, traditional architecture, and an atmosphere that also suits hanbok

Touring actual wedding venues would be best. If that is not possible, I think it helps to develop a sense of the atmosphere you want through Instagram and various blogs.
