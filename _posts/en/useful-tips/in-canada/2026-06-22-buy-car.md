Commonly used websites when buying a used car in BC include Canadian Black Book, Kelly Blue Book, and Auto Trader. Other options include Kijiji, Craiglist, and Facebook Marketplace.

You may have to pay a processing fee if you purchase with a credit card.

Be sure to take a test drive and check the repair and accident history, as well as how many times ownership has changed.

You can easily check this through Carfax or CarPoorf using the VIN, or vehicle identification number.

Other things to check include whether the windows carry the manufacturer's mark, the air conditioning, wipers, doors, and so on.

You must have insurance through ICBC, BC's designated insurer.

If you buy from a dealer, you may have to pay a doc fee, admin fee, finance placement fee, and other charges.

- Doc fee

- Admin fee

- Finance placement fee

- Reconditioning fee

- Market adjustment

- Paint/rust/fabric protection

- Tire

- Extended warranty

- GAP insurance

You may get a good price through a dealership's end-of-month promotion. Communicating by email is a good option, too.

Choose a dealer certified on Auto Trader.

Vehicle purchases are subject to 12% tax: 12% PST for a private sale, or 5% GST and 7% PST when buying from a dealership. Additional tax applies to luxury vehicles costing $55,000 or more.
