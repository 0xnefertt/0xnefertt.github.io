> This post provides general information as of July 22, 2026, and is not personalized tax or investment advice. Limits and eligibility may change from year to year or depending on individual circumstances, so check your CRA account and consult a tax professional before filing or contributing.

In Canada, individuals generally file an income tax return each year. Filing and payment deadlines vary depending on personal circumstances, self-employment status, and other factors, so it is safest to check the [CRA's tax filing deadlines](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes/income-tax/personal-income-tax/get-ready-taxes/dates.html) first.

## The Basic Structure of Registered Accounts

| Account | General Tax Treatment                                                                                                                    | What to Check First                                                                       |
| ------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| TFSA    | Contributions are not tax-deductible, but interest, dividends, capital gains within the account, and withdrawals are generally tax-free. | Your individual contribution room and whether you have overcontributed.                   |
| FHSA    | Contributions by eligible first-time home buyers are generally deductible, and qualifying withdrawals are tax-free.                      | First-time home buyer eligibility, participation limits, and when the account must close. |
| RRSP    | Contributions can be deducted within your available limit, and income earned in the account is generally tax-deferred until withdrawal.  | Your deduction limit, taxation on withdrawal, and conversion rules at maturity.           |
| RESP    | Contributions themselves are not deductible, but education savings can be invested alongside government grants.                          | Beneficiary eligibility, grants, and tax treatment of each component of withdrawals.      |

A TFSA is not an account intended for just one investment approach. It can hold cash, GICs, or permitted investments, and tax issues may arise in exceptional situations, such as frequent trading being deemed a business activity. Rather than assuming it is "always suitable for short-term investing," you should choose an account based on your goals, time horizon, and risk tolerance.

Maximizing contributions to an FHSA or RRSP is not necessarily the best choice for everyone either. Emergency funds, debt costs, current and expected retirement tax rates, and home-buying plans should all be considered together.

## Credits and Business Expenses

The GST/HST credit, Canada Child Benefit, Disability Tax Credit, and Canada Workers Benefit each have their own eligibility requirements based on income and family circumstances. Do not assume you qualify just from the name; check the CRA's eligibility guidance.

Self-employed people can generally deduct reasonable current expenses incurred to earn income, but cannot deduct personal expenses. For costs that combine personal and business use, separate out only the business portion and retain supporting records such as receipts, contracts, and usage logs. Separate CCA rules may apply to capital expenditures.
