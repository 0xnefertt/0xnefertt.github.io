In Canada, education falls under provincial rather than federal jurisdiction. School systems and registration procedures therefore vary somewhat between BC, Ontario, and Alberta. Public-school placement is generally based on a student's residential address. The area of addresses assigned to a particular school is called a school catchment or catchment area. Each region has a school district or school board that manages its schools, and student registration follows that authority's procedures. Living within a catchment area can give you priority for enrollment at that school, but admission is not always guaranteed, depending on capacity and registration timing.

If you want to send your child to a good school district, there are only three options:

1. Move into the catchment area.

2. Submit a cross-boundary / cross-district application.

3. Apply through a special program.

In BC, I would say with confidence that West Vancouver is an area with good schools. For one thing, it has high housing prices and a high cost of living. It is similar to Seoul's Gangnam School District 8, although the atmosphere is quite different. You might wonder, "How does that make it a good school district?" People here do not place as much emphasis on school districts, but if you were to compare them, the public schools themselves have a good reputation. It is said that roughly 97–98% of students graduate each year, and over 87% of graduates go on to a post-secondary institution. The area also has private/independent schools that prepare students for university through programs such as IB and AP.
