Every New Year, I've set goals for the year ahead.

In 2019:

- Establish my values
- The people around me

In 2020:

- Health: keep up with the gym, swimming, and looking after my body
- Volunteer work
- Reading: 30 books
- Be good to my parents
- Dating

Unfortunately, I have no record for 2021.

In 2022, I wrote a bucket list of things I wanted to do in my twenties.

- Get waxed
- Take a family portrait
- Resign/change jobs
- 100 million won
- Travel abroad or travel alone
- Stay at a five-star hotel
- Join a club

In 2023, the goals seem to have evolved into something more specific.

Personal development goals

- Read 10 books
- Earn one certification: Computer Specialist in Spreadsheet & Database, Level 1
- Complete my public-speaking classes
- Move up a level at my English academy
- Finish all the Udemy courses I've bought: Rust, Golang, TypeScript, Git...
- Reach 1,000 Twitter followers

Habits

- Exercise at least once a week
- Consistently do forward-head-posture stretches every morning
- Arrive at work early: one hour before the scheduled time
- Wash up within an hour of getting home
- Clean up immediately after eating
- Reduce unnecessary time on social media: TikTok, Instagram, YouTube

Finance

- I'd like to create one source of side income

Other

- If I change jobs, don't let the break between them exceed one month

In 2024, I wrote a reflection in Notion, looking back on my twenties and what I'd done well and poorly. I think that was when I began writing reflections rather than New Year's plans. My annual goals seem to be almost the same each year, and I achieve about half while failing at the rest. So now I tend to use the New Year simply to reset my mindset rather than make another set of goals.

I recently posted my reflection on 2025 on the blog. At the end, I briefly mentioned my plans for this year, 2026. Nothing grand—just taking care of my health.

This year's goals won't be much different from before: I plan to focus on routine. Ever since I saw the phrase, "**If you keep doing it, it will become familiar; once it's familiar, you'll naturally get good at it,**" I've come to believe that "**routine is everything.**"

Separately, I think the biggest event will probably be preparing for our wedding.
