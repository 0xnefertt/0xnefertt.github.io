I feel as though I have become someone who finds it difficult to do anything without AI. Turn that statement around, though, and it also means that with AI, I feel I could try anything. Of course, I probably cannot fully match someone with advanced skills. But given time, I think I could at least imitate their methods to some extent. That is why I believe overconfidence is the attitude we must be most careful about when using AI.

AI is becoming an extension of the smartphone. In the past, producing something meaningful on the internet meant sitting at a desk and spending a long time on it. Now, even without a laptop, we can code remotely using just a smartphone, and the results are much better than before. AI helps me do what I am good at even better, and enables me to do things I cannot do on my own. In some ways, it feels all-purpose. But while it can find efficient answers to choice-based questions such as “Should I do A or B?”, it does not first tell me what I truly want.

For now, AI remains something we use. It is a tool that appears when we need empathy, calculations, or help and fulfills our requirements. But people must ultimately decide what they want, where to go, and which problems to solve. That is why sound or creative thinking is necessary. With the arrival of AI, the cost of production is moving closer and closer to zero. In the future, as data repeatedly becomes more distributed and scattered or consolidates into just one or two surviving super-apps, the gap will grow wider.
