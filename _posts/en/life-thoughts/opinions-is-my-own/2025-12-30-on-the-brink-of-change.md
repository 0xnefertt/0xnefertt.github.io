Ten years ago, security specialists ranked among the most promising careers. Just five years ago, developer jobs that allowed remote work became hugely popular during the COVID era. Even a year ago, AI—artificial intelligence—wasn't the talking point it is today. I think that was because the writing, images, and source code ChatGPT produced always seemed to lack something. As time passes, AI's capabilities are growing at an astonishing rate. Now I've reached the point where I actually trust AI tools and struggle without them.

As everyone knows, the AI era is coming. Or perhaps it has already arrived.

As industries advance and paradigms shift, some jobs disappear and new ones emerge. In other words, this is a signal that someone may be facing a golden opportunity.

I often wonder, "What jobs can't AI replace?" and "What can only humans do?" I believe that even jobs requiring people to physically use their bodies, such as plumbers, electricians, and hairdressers, could eventually be replaced by machines. After all, every invention so far has come into being to make human tasks—especially repetitive ones—more convenient.

That's why I feel thinking is the most important thing.

People say AI can think for itself, but ultimately all it does is read and learn from data quickly. (At least, that seems to be the case for now.) It looks as though it creates something new, but much of it is similar to what already exists. In the end, thinking is a uniquely human act. We should strive to be people who think creatively.

If technology ever gave us the superpower to read someone's thoughts, as we've seen in films, I feel society would truly become lifeless.

AI is a form of computing,

and what computers do best is repetition.

They can work 24 hours a day without becoming tired, burning out, or getting bored.

Humans, on the other hand, can't do that, so we inevitably fall behind.

Faced with this, we can't help feeling both helplessness and loss.

I don't know what significance writing like this will have in the future.

It might serve only as personal note-taking.

This post, too, was written to organize and record my thoughts.
