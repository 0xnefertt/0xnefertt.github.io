The weekend went by so quickly that I can barely remember what I did. Weekends always seem to be like that. They always feel short.

Before I turned twenty and became an adult, weekends weren't entirely my own time. I probably spent a lot of them with my parents or friends. But as a kid, I loved online games like MapleStory, Dungeon Fighter Online, and League of Legends. I was so obsessed that I'd go to PC cafés alone. Maybe that helped me become someone who's comfortable on their own; even now, I rarely feel bored or at a loose end.

Of course, I don't play games anymore. As a student, I couldn't quit even after hearing my tutor tell me dozens of times that "games do nothing but harm." But after finishing my military service, I stopped completely. Looking back, it still amazes me. It really does seem that men need to do their military service to get a wake-up call.

Anyway, yesterday YouTube's recommendations happened to lead me to the following videos and posts, and they made me worried about the future.

[https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UiPjwJEoey4](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UiPjwJEoey4)

["Terrifying and shocking": Shenzhen, the world's most advanced IT city, living in the year 2100 🇨🇳](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UiPjwJEoey4)

The first was an introduction to Shenzhen, China's city of the future. Self-driving taxis, drone deliveries—everything ran on AI, and people lived conveniently.

At the same time, I felt worried about Korea. I began wondering whether the things that had allowed Korea to prosper—brands such as Samsung and LG, and its strength in IT—might become a little precarious in the future.

As the comments pointed out, it's true that Korea's geniuses and top students prefer medical school to engineering.

Korea's future may lie in promising areas such as beauty or entertainment.

One other thing that surprised me was the thought that perhaps I'd been far too narrow-minded. My image of China had been overwhelmingly one of noise, rudeness, and fear, but I began thinking that might not be right anymore.

Maybe our habit of learning English and endlessly trusting and following the United States as the number-one country could be mistaken.

[https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d95J8yzvjbQ](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d95J8yzvjbQ)

[The Thinking Game | Full documentary | Tribeca Film Festival official selection](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d95J8yzvjbQ)

The second video was a documentary a little over an hour long featuring Google DeepMind's CEO Demis Hassabis and co-founder Shane Legg.

All I could say was, "Incredible."

Lee Sedol appears in it, and I felt another surge of Korean pride. It was the first time I'd learned that the AlphaGo team that played Go against Lee Sedol was also DeepMind.

And I wondered whether I should have jumped into AI back then.

I thought the AI era might arrive someday—or perhaps very soon—and immediately worried about what I should be doing.

[No. 1 in Korean beauty market capitalization in just 10 years 🔥 APR's formula for success that every founder should know | Kim Byung-hoon, APR CEO | 2025 Chung Ju-yung Startup Competition](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q_NgaXK0l6c)

[https://blog.naver.com/mynameisdj/224102591736](https://blog.naver.com/mynameisdj/224102591736)

[How Does Elon Musk Work?](https://blog.naver.com/mynameisdj/224102591736)

The last item is a post about Elon Musk.

Reading and watching all this over the weekend only left me wondering and worrying about what kind of person I am and how I should live from here...

Unlike those people, I haven't had some extraordinary drive and ambition to change the world since childhood. I don't think I even wanted to.

From now on, I should pursue activities that challenge me and bring a little more energy into my life...
