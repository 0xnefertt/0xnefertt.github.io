Not easy.

Before starting a blog, you've probably imagined it at least once: writing posts, making money, getting advertising offers, and working hard at it as a side hustle. You may have searched for monetized blogs, decorated your own, and tried to write consistently. Anyone who has ever blogged knows it isn't easy. So I'm writing this to organize my thoughts on how we should approach blogging.

1\. **Write about things other people are curious about, rather than simply what you want to write.** The hardest thing about starting a blog is having to write without knowing who your readers are. You need to think carefully about the tone and level of your writing. If you want a topic that most people will be curious about regardless of who they are, write something timely.

2\. However, timely posts can become buried as time passes. If views are your main focus, writing breaking-news-style posts may be useful, but you'll soon wear yourself out. Writing becomes tiring and exhausting, making it hard to keep going for long. And because anyone can run this kind of labor-intensive blog, it's not easy to survive.

3\. Even so, it's worth continually thinking about which topics are timely and likely to attract interest. If you find something you feel capable of writing about, use the knowledge you have to produce a thoughtful post quickly.

4\. As you steadily accumulate good posts, there will eventually be a moment when one ranks highly in search results or gets shared and takes off. While waiting for that moment, keep stocking your blog with good writing. That's why the most important part of blogging is to keep writing consistently at a reasonable level of quality.

5\. I think the era of making money purely from blog views has passed. Of course, some people still do, but aiming to earn from page views when you're just starting is a somewhat old-fashioned approach to running a blog.

6\. Use your blog to build a brand around yourself. You can learn a lot in the process: what topics people care about, how they search before finding you, why your own view counts are low, and so on. Only when you seriously think about your blog can you begin to grow.

7\. Rather than just writing posts, I'm thinking about where to put them so people will actually see them. With Google search acting strangely these days, I've been wondering how to get my posts near the top of its results.

8\. Tistory definitely has a very different atmosphere from Naver. People don't build friendships by adding blog neighbors, and there are fewer topic categories showcasing Tistory posts than on Naver.

9\. The usual advice for creating an optimized blog is to choose one topic and upload only content related to it, but I'm not sure that works on Tistory. (If the aim is maximum exposure, wouldn't it make more sense to write quickly, regardless of the topic...?) Posts need to be easy to read and coherent while attracting as much attention as possible.

10\. Coming back to the topic, here's how to definitely make money from a blog: 1) write things people will find interesting, 2) write consistently, 3) steadily collect good posts on your blog, and 4) write timely posts too. Still not making money after all that? Even so, try it for just 100 days, and you'll gain experience worth more than money.

11\. Let's all start blogging. With a Lucky Vicky mindset.
