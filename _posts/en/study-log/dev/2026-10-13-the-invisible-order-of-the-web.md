An organization above another organization, and another above that. We live in a system of visible organizations intertwined with layers of invisible order. That system is woven deeply into our daily lives. Even today, when historical status systems have been abolished and equality is taken for granted, hierarchies between people and organizations have not disappeared. A company, for example, has teams, departments, and executives. A country also has many institutions with different powers and responsibilities.

The same kind of system exists online. Behind the websites we use without much thought are the people who develop and operate them. Behind those people are the teams building browsers and communications technologies, along with organizations creating standards so different technologies can work together. As users, we may not know what technologies are being used or what happens behind the scenes, yet we enjoy many benefits. And for free, too. That is a wonderful thing.

I do not know how far up the web’s hierarchy goes, but I decided to write this post to explore as high as I could.

The five organizations above are prominent examples of bodies playing important roles in the standards and operating systems of the web and internet. Many other organizations work in their respective fields, collaborating to make different technologies compatible and advance them.
