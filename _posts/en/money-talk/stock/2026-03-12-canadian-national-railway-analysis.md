The following is a brief overview of the Canadian company Canadian National Railway (ticker: CNI, NYSE/TSX).

## 1. Company Overview

- Founded: 1919 as Canada's national railway; privatized in 1995
- Headquarters: Montreal, Quebec
- Core business: Operating a transcontinental North American rail freight network
- Major assets: Approximately 32,000 km of rail lines across Canada and connecting the U.S. Midwest and South
- Official website: https://www.cn.ca/

Canadian National Railway is Canada's largest railway company and part of North America's essential supply chain. It provides integrated logistics solutions for grain, minerals, energy products, consumer goods, and industrial products. Its distinctive three-coast network connecting the east and west coasts with the Gulf of Mexico is its core competitive strength.

## 1.1. Investment Characteristics

- Infrastructure characteristics (30%): Stable cash flow based on an exclusive route network
- Cyclical characteristics (25%): Directly linked to industrial production and trade volumes
- Dividend-stock characteristics (15%): Consistent dividend growth supported by stable cash flow
- Value-stock characteristics (15%): Can be valued based on assets and cash-generating ability
- Growth-stock characteristics (10%): Margin expansion through operating improvements and price increases
- Thematic characteristics (5%): Exposure to intra-North American trade and supply chain restructuring

## 1.2. Core Business Structure

- Freight transportation: Transporting diverse categories, including intermodal freight, raw materials, and consumer goods
- Network efficiency: Optimizing on-time delivery, fuel efficiency, and asset utilization
- Additional services: Logistics solutions, warehousing, and supply chain management

## 2. Background and Management

- Background: Established by the Canadian government in 1919 as Canadian National Railways through the consolidation of several bankrupt railways. It was fully privatized in 1995 and now operates in its present form.
- Management focus: Prefers strategies emphasizing operating efficiency, safety management, and customer service quality.
- CEO: Tracy Robinson

## 3. Products and Revenue Structure

- Intermodal transportation: Containers and trailers, approximately 45% of revenue
- Commodity transportation: Grain, fertilizer, petroleum, and chemicals, approximately 30% of revenue
- Consumer and industrial goods: Finished products, automotive parts, paper products, and more, approximately 25% of revenue

Revenue primarily consists of freight charges based on volume and distance. Network optimization and asset utilization are central to profitability.

## 4. Market and Competitive Environment

High barriers to entry make the North American railway industry an oligopoly. Canadian National Railway and Canadian Pacific Kansas City (CP) form the main Canadian duopoly, while partially competing with Class I railways in the United States. New entry into this infrastructure-based business is effectively impossible, making service quality and reliability the primary competitive factors.

## 5. Growth Drivers

- Increasing intra-North American trade and the need to optimize supply chains
- Growing intermodal demand due to cost and environmental efficiency relative to trucking
- Operating improvements through Precision Scheduled Railroading
- Increasing transportation of specialized cargo for the energy transition, such as wind turbine components and solar panels
- Greater access to South American markets through Mexico

## 6. Key Risks

- Lower industrial production and trade volumes during recessions
- Strikes and labor negotiation risks due to high union representation
- Transportation disruptions from extreme weather, including severe cold and flooding
- Regulatory changes involving safety and environmental standards
- Competition from alternative transportation, including long-haul trucking and shipping

## 7. Points to Watch

- Freight volume trends, analyzed by category
- Improvements in the operating ratio
- Capital efficiency, including asset turnover and ROIC
- Safety indicators, including accident and employee injury rates
- Intermodal terminal utilization and expansion plans

## Conclusion

Canadian National Railway acts as an artery of North America's essential supply chain through its distinctive three-coast rail network. Although it is economically sensitive, its infrastructure exclusivity supports stable cash generation, and operating improvements offer opportunities for margin expansion. These are central to long-term growth. Supply chain restructuring and increasing demand for greener logistics may also provide favorable external conditions.
