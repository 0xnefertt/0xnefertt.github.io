The following is a brief overview of the Canadian company Canadian Pacific Kansas City (ticker: CP, NYSE/TSX).

## 1. Company Overview

- Founded: 2021 through the merger of Canadian Pacific and Kansas City Southern, whose histories date to 1881 and 1887, respectively
- Headquarters: Calgary, Alberta
- Core business: Operating a transcontinental North American rail freight network connecting Canada, the central and southern United States, and Mexico
- Major assets: Approximately 32,000 km of rail lines connecting Western Canada with the U.S. Midwest and South and Mexico
- Official website: https://www.cpr.ca/

Canadian Pacific Kansas City (CP) operates the first and only single railway network connecting Western Canada, the U.S. Midwest and South, and Mexico. It provides integrated logistics solutions for grain, energy products, intermodal freight, automobiles, and consumer goods. Access to South American markets through Mexico is a distinctive competitive strength.

## 1.1. Investment Characteristics

- Infrastructure characteristics (30%): Stable cash flow based on an exclusive route network
- Cyclical characteristics (25%): Directly linked to industrial production and trade volumes, particularly automobiles and industrial goods
- Dividend-stock characteristics (15%): Consistent dividend growth supported by stable cash flow
- Value-stock characteristics (15%): Can be valued based on assets and cash-generating ability
- Growth-stock characteristics (10%): Margin expansion through operating improvements and price increases
- Thematic characteristics (5%): Exposure to intra-North American trade, supply chain restructuring, and access to South American markets through Mexico

## 1.2. Core Business Structure

- Freight transportation: Transporting diverse categories, including intermodal freight, raw materials, and consumer goods
- Network efficiency: Optimizing on-time delivery, fuel efficiency, and asset utilization
- Additional services: Logistics solutions, warehousing, supply chain management, and single-carrier service

## 2. Background and Management

- Background: Established in 2021 through the merger of Canadian Pacific (CP) and Kansas City Southern (K), whose histories date to 1881 and 1887, respectively. The merger completed a single-carrier network connecting Western Canada with Mexico.
- Management focus: Prefers strategies emphasizing operating efficiency, safety management, and customer service quality.
- CEO: Keith Creel

## 3. Products and Revenue Structure

- Intermodal transportation: Containers and trailers, approximately 45% of revenue
- Commodity transportation: Grain, fertilizer, petroleum, and chemicals, approximately 30% of revenue
- Consumer and industrial goods: Finished products, automotive parts, paper products, and more, approximately 25% of revenue

Revenue primarily consists of freight charges based on volume and distance. Network optimization and asset utilization are central to profitability. A particular strength is that single-carrier service can reduce transfer costs and time.

## 4. Market and Competitive Environment

High barriers to entry make the North American railway industry an oligopoly. Canadian National Railway (CNI) and Canadian Pacific Kansas City (CP) form the main Canadian duopoly, while partially competing with Class I railways in the United States. New entry into this infrastructure-based business is effectively impossible, making service quality and reliability the primary competitive factors. CP's distinctive advantage is a single-carrier network from Western Canada to Mexico; competitors can offer similar routes only through transfers.

## 5. Growth Drivers

- Increasing intra-North American trade and the need to optimize supply chains
- Growing intermodal demand due to cost and environmental efficiency relative to trucking
- Operating improvements through Precision Scheduled Railroading
- Increasing transportation of specialized cargo for the energy transition, such as wind turbine components and solar panels
- Greater access to South American markets through Mexico, linked to growing automotive and manufacturing investment
- Better logistics efficiency and customer value through single-carrier service

## 6. Key Risks

- Lower industrial production and trade volumes during recessions, with particular sensitivity to automobiles and industrial goods
- Strikes and labor negotiation risks due to high union representation
- Transportation disruptions from extreme weather, including severe cold and flooding
- Regulatory changes involving safety and environmental standards
- Competition from alternative transportation, including long-haul trucking and shipping
- Exposure to South American economic fluctuations through reliance on freight routed through Mexico

## 7. Points to Watch

- Freight volume trends, analyzed by category
- Improvements in the operating ratio
- Capital efficiency, including asset turnover and ROIC
- Safety indicators, including accident and employee injury rates
- Intermodal terminal utilization and expansion plans
- The share and growth of freight routed through Mexico
- Adoption of single-carrier service and its impact on profitability

## Conclusion

Canadian Pacific Kansas City plays a central role in North America's essential supply chain through its distinctive single-carrier rail network connecting Western Canada, the central and southern United States, and Mexico. Despite economic sensitivity, stable cash generation from infrastructure exclusivity, potential margin expansion through operating improvements, and access to South American markets through Mexico are key long-term growth variables. Supply chain restructuring and rising demand for greener logistics may also provide favorable external conditions. Greater logistics efficiency through single-carrier service offers a distinctive competitive advantage.
