Income categories follow the **Income Tax Act**.

An individual’s income is classified into eight categories.

Corporations have a separate Corporate Tax Act.

There are also the Value-Added Tax Act, Inheritance Tax and Gift Tax Act, Local Tax Act, and various other tax laws.

For now, let’s start with personal income.

1\. Interest income: Money received in exchange for providing your money to others (interest from deposits, installment savings, bonds, etc.).

2\. Dividend income: Income received in return for investing in a company’s capital (dividends and distributions).

3\. Business income: Income generated by continuously and repeatedly carrying on a business.

3.3% = Income tax of 3% + Local income tax of 0.3%

4\. Employment income: Income received for providing labor under an employment relationship (monthly salary, annual salary, bonuses, performance pay, etc.).

Tax already deducted when you receive your salary is withholding tax.

Recalculating the exact amount at year-end is the year-end tax settlement.

5\. Pension income: Money received as a pension (National Pension, civil-service pensions, private-school teachers’ pensions, military pensions, pension savings, and certain IRP pension payments).

6\. Other income: Specific types of income defined in the Income Tax Act.

\- Prize money

\- Bounties

\- Rewards

\- Occasional lecture fees

\- Manuscript fees

\- Certain royalties

\- Honoraria

\- Compensation for services provided on a one-off basis

\- Religious workers’ income

(\* Whether an activity is continuous and repeated is the most important factor in determining the category.)

**The six categories above are collectively called comprehensive income. (Comprehensive income tax)**

**The remaining categories—7. Retirement income and 8. Capital gains—are called separately assessed income.**

7\. Retirement income: Money received upon retirement (retirement/severance pay).

8\. Capital gains (capital gains tax): Income generated by transferring ownership of assets.

\- Land

\- Buildings

\- Apartments

\- Presale rights

\- Rights relating to real estate

\- Certain shares

\- Other assets specified by law

When we sell assets like these, we must pay capital gains tax.

---

**Interest and dividend income** are generally taxed at 15.4%.

**Business and employment income:** Korea’s basic comprehensive income tax rates are divided into eight progressive brackets, ranging from 6% to 45%, depending on taxable income.

The important point is this:

If your annual salary is KRW 100 million, you do not pay 35% on the entire KRW 100 million.

It is a **progressive tax**, with 6%, 15%, 24%, and 35% applied successively to the respective brackets.

And **individual local income tax is generally added separately at 10% of the income tax amount.**

In practice, the combined rates are approximately 6.6%, 16.5%, 26.4%, 38.5% … up to 49.5%.

For pension income, the rate varies by age and pension type.

Other income is basically taxed at 20% + 2%.

Retirement income and capital gains are calculated separately.

Capital gains can be taxed at up to 70%, making this one of the more complicated areas.
