Today, let’s learn about the economy.

**The economy refers to all activities involved in producing, distributing, and using the goods and services people need to live, along with the social relationships they create.** The Korean term is **an abbreviation of the classical phrase “gyeongguk jemin”—governing the country and helping the people.**

1\. The three elements of economic activity = production, distribution, and consumption

2\. Economic agents = households, businesses, and government

3\. Factors of production = **labor, land, and capital**

**The economy’s basic structure is a repeating cycle: “factors of production → production → income distribution → consumption → production again”** (※ saving → investment may also be added here)**.**

4\. Core economic principles = scarcity, choice, and opportunity cost

**Economics studies how to make efficient choices and allocate scarce resources.**

**“What should be produced?”**

**“How should it be produced?”**

**“For whom should it be produced?”**

5\. Economic thought

Ancient and medieval times → Mercantilism (16th–18th centuries) → Physiocracy → Classical economics → Socialism and Marx → Marginal revolution, neoclassical economics, and the Austrian school → Keynesian school → Chicago school and monetarism → Modern economics

The key here is that the question changes with each era.

Early period — “Are money and commerce morally justified?”

Early modern period — “How does a nation become wealthy?”

From classical economics onward — “How does the market function on its own?”

After the Industrial Revolution — “How can capitalism’s distribution problem be resolved?”

20th century — “Should the government intervene during a downturn?”

Today — “Under which circumstances are markets or governments more efficient?”

7\. Microeconomics and macroeconomics

**Microeconomics**

→ Supply and demand → Elasticity → Consumers → Firms → Market structure → Market failure

**Macroeconomics**

→ GDP → Prices → Unemployment → Business cycles → Interest rates → Monetary policy → Fiscal policy → Exchange rates → Economic growth

8\. Finance and international economics

**International economics**

→ Comparative advantage → Free trade → Tariffs and protectionism → Exchange rates → Balance of payments → Capital flows → International monetary system

**Financial economics**

→ Money and financial markets → Stocks and bonds → Interest rates → Risk and return → Asset prices → Portfolios → Derivatives → Financial crises
